01 · The Exam

A benchmark built by the climate community, to test AI.

Earth's Last Exam asks one question: can today's most capable AI models actually solve complex climate and environmental problems?

Inspired by Humanity's Last Exam, it narrows the focus from general academic knowledge to the science, impacts, mitigation, adaptation and policy questions that climate researchers and decision-makers work on every day.

We are collecting about 500 closed-ended questions from leading scientists, policymakers, and anyone else from around the world who thinks they know enough about climate science to stump frontier AI. Each question has a single verifiable answer, so AI models can be scored objectively.

02 · Why it matters

You don't have to like AI to care how well it performs.

AI tools are already answering public questions about climate change, and they are being considered for drafting mitigation and adaptation plans and supporting international negotiations. Whether that helps or harms depends on something few people have measured rigorously: where does AI get environmental science right, and where does it fail?

Science communication

Chatbots increasingly field public questions about climate. A confident error can travel faster than the correction.

Planning and policy

Governments and advisers are exploring AI to help design and carry out mitigation and adaptation plans. Decisions on emissions, infrastructure and risk need evidence of reliability.

Global negotiations

As AI enters the preparation and analysis around climate talks, a subtle error in a number or a treaty can carry real consequences.

A credible test needs people who know where the hard problems are, and it needs skeptics: your questions are how we find the limits of AI, and how we show where human expertise remains essential.

If AI is going to inform climate decisions, it should first have to pass a test written by the people who understand climate science best.

04 · Contribute

What we're looking for.

Anyone can contribute, and you do not need to be an expert. We are especially hoping to hear from people with deep experience in climate or the environment, because they are the ones who will ask the questions that stump AI. Students, practitioners, researchers and curious generalists are all welcome.

Prizes. The best questions will win prizes of up to $12,000. Details to come.

You can begin thinking now. A strong question is:

  • Closed-ended. One verifiable answer, such as a number, a term or a choice.
  • Challenging. It takes real reasoning, ideally drawn from your own field, not a quick web search.
  • Unambiguous. It does not hinge on opinion or contested value judgments.
  • Grounded. It reflects a real problem in climate science, impacts, mitigation, adaptation or policy.

Preliminary guidance. Formal criteria will be published when submissions open.

06 · FAQ

Common questions.

Who can contribute?

Anyone. You do not need to be an expert. We are targeting people with experience in climate science, environmental science, economics, policy and related fields because they tend to write the hardest and best questions, but good questions can come from anywhere. Final eligibility details will be shared before submissions open.

How is this different from Humanity's Last Exam?

Humanity's Last Exam tests broad academic knowledge across many fields. Earth's Last Exam focuses on complex problems related to climate change, with questions drawn from the work scientists, planners and policymakers actually do.

I'm skeptical of AI. Why should I take part?

Because rigorous, independent testing is how we learn where AI can and cannot be trusted. Skeptics are exactly who we need: you know where the hard problems are.

Will contributors be credited?

We plan to recognize contributors. Details on credit and prizes (up to $12,000 for the best questions) will be announced with the submission guidelines.

07 · About

An independent research initiative.

Earth's Last Exam is run by the Data-Driven EnviroLab (DDL), an interdisciplinary research collective at UNC-Chapel Hill's Institute for the Environment. DDL's mission is to turn complex environmental data into real-world solutions.

Our previous work with AI:

  • ChatNetZero: an AI assistant that helps people interpret climate commitments by governments and companies and judge whether a net-zero target is credible. It draws on expert-vetted data from the Net Zero Tracker and discloses the energy used for each answer.
  • Developing and Assessing Credible AI Workflows for Decarbonization Insights: a DDL learning document (October 2026) on building an AI-assisted workflow to draft 12 low-carbon sourcing playbooks and testing the output with structured expert review. AI's own quality judgments matched the final human judgments only 44% of the time, and most errors were unsupported claims and sourcing problems rather than outright fabrication.
  • U.S. Data Center Policy Explorer: an interactive map of more than 1,500 U.S. data center campuses alongside hundreds of state policy records, showing how states are regulating the industry.
  • Climate AI for Cities: a dashboard and database cataloging AI solutions for city climate mitigation, adaptation and energy access, each assessed on technology readiness, scale, open-source availability and institutional feasibility.
  • Report Scout: a tool that monitors investor-relations pages and official sources for corporate annual, ESG and sustainability reports and emails analysts verified updates.
